# Mental-health support resources for students in Vietnam

## Helplines and emergency support

- **Ngay Mai helpline**: 096 306 1414 — free psychological support for young
  people in crisis (operates at set hours during the week).
- **National child protection hotline 111**: free, 24/7 — supports young people
  under 18 and can refer callers onwards.
- **Medical emergency 115**: when there is a threat to life.
- If you have thoughts of harming yourself: **do not stay on your own** — contact
  someone you trust immediately, call one of the numbers above, or go to the
  nearest medical facility.

## University counselling offices

Many universities in Vietnam have a counselling office or centre offering free
psychological support to students (usually under the Student Affairs Office or the
Youth Union). Students should:

1. Look for information on their own Student Affairs Office website or page.
2. Ask an academic advisor or class supervisor about the university's mental-health
   support channels.
3. Book an appointment — what you discuss is kept confidential under professional
   ethics rules.

## Specialist clinical services

- **National Institute of Mental Health** — Bach Mai Hospital, Hanoi.
- **Central Psychiatric Hospital 1** (Thuong Tin, Hanoi) and **Central Psychiatric
  Hospital 2** (Bien Hoa, Dong Nai).
- **Ho Chi Minh City Psychiatric Hospital**; psychology and psychiatry departments
  at provincial and city general hospitals.
- Private psychology clinics and online counselling platforms with Vietnamese
  practitioners.

## Things to know when seeking support

- Mental-health care at public facilities using health insurance may be partially
  covered under current regulations.
- Feeling awkward about going for an appointment is normal; mental health is like
  physical health — catching things early makes the response gentler and more
  effective.
- If the first person you talk to is not the right fit, do not give up — try
  another support channel.
