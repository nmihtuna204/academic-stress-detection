# Coping with family pressure, money worries and social comparison

## Pressure from family expectations

In Vietnamese culture, a child's academic achievement is often bound up with the
pride of the whole family. This can motivate, but it easily becomes a burden ("I'm
afraid of letting my parents down", "other people's children"). Some ways to cope:

- **Have a prepared, honest conversation**: pick a calm moment and explain
  specifically what your programme involves, how hard it is, and what your plans
  are; parents often base expectations on an outdated picture of university and
  will adjust once they understand the reality.
- **Set goals based on your own values**: separate "what I want" from "what others
  want for me"; set achievable academic goals matched to your abilities and a
  realistic career direction.
- **Share progress regularly**: update your family on both effort and difficulties
  rather than only reporting end-of-term results — this reduces the pressure of
  being judged purely on grades.

## Financial pressure and part-time work

- Build a simple monthly budget (rent, food, study costs, a buffer) and track
  spending with an app or a notebook.
- Look into **scholarships, tuition waivers and student loans** through the Student
  Affairs Office — many support schemes are little known.
- If you work part-time: prioritise flexible hours close to your campus or home;
  cap your total hours (roughly under 20 a week) so work does not eat into study
  and sleep.
- Work related to your field (teaching assistance, paid internships, freelance work
  in your discipline) provides income and experience at the same time.

## Social comparison and social media

- Remember that social media is a highlight reel: people post successes and rarely
  post failures — comparing yourself with that image is an unfair comparison.
- Limit scrolling time (set app timers); unfollow accounts that make you feel bad
  about yourself.
- Practise "vertical comparison": compare yourself today with yourself last
  semester, rather than with other people.
- Write down 3 things you managed each week — the brain has a negativity bias, so
  progress needs to be recorded deliberately.
