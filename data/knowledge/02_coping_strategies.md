# Strategies for coping with academic stress

## Group 1: Time and study management

- **Break tasks down**: split a large project or assignment into 30–60 minute
  steps; completing each step creates a sense of progress and reduces the feeling
  of being overwhelmed.
- **The Pomodoro technique**: study for 25 minutes, break for 5; after 4 cycles
  take a longer 15–30 minute break. Useful when you struggle to focus or tend to
  procrastinate.
- **The Eisenhower matrix**: sort tasks by importance and urgency to prioritise
  correctly; learn to decline what is not important.
- **Plan your week**: spend 15 minutes on Sunday evening listing deadlines, exams
  and classes for the week; this avoids last-minute panic.
- **Start with the easiest thing**: when you feel stuck, do the simplest part
  first to break the inertia of procrastination.

## Group 2: Looking after your body

- **Sleep**: keep consistent bed and wake times, aiming for 7–8 hours a night;
  avoid screens 30 minutes before bed; no caffeine after 3pm. Sleep deprivation
  markedly reduces memory and increases the stress response.
- **Exercise**: 20–30 minutes a day (brisk walking, running, cycling, badminton,
  the gym); exercise releases endorphins and lowers the stress hormone cortisol.
- **Eat regularly**: do not skip meals during exam periods; limit energy drinks;
  drink enough water.

## Group 3: Regulating emotions

- **4-7-8 breathing**: breathe in for 4 seconds, hold for 7, breathe out for 8,
  repeat 4 times — useful right before an exam or when you feel keyed up.
- **Box breathing**: in for 4 seconds, hold 4, out 4, hold 4; repeat for 5 minutes.
- **Journalling**: writing down what is stressing you and how you reacted helps
  get the thoughts out of your head and see the problem more objectively.
- **Mindfulness**: spend 5–10 minutes observing your breath; a meditation app can
  help.
- **Limit social media**: set specific time windows; constantly comparing yourself
  with other people's images of success increases anxiety.

## Group 4: Connection and social support

- **Talk to someone you trust**: telling a close friend, an older sibling or your
  parents about your difficulties — simply being heard measurably reduces stress.
- **Study groups**: revising with friends helps maintain motivation and reduces
  the sense of being alone.
- **Extracurricular activities in moderation**: clubs and volunteering broaden
  your relationships, but keep them balanced so they do not become a new source of
  overload.
- **Seek professional support**: your university counselling office, a
  psychologist or a doctor — especially when self-management is no longer working.

## A note on harmful coping

Avoid: repeatedly pulling all-nighters to catch up, overusing caffeine and energy
drinks, drinking alcohol to "let off steam", skipping meals, isolating yourself, or
missing exams to avoid them. These reduce tension briefly but make the problem
worse in the long run.
